Privacy policy

Robin & Coco · Last updated: 21 November 2026

In short: Robin & Coco collects nothing. No accounts, no ads, no analytics, no tracking. The one thing that ever leaves your phone is in the phone app, and only if you turn Robin's weather on: a place, rounded to about a kilometre, sent to Open-Meteo for the weather there.

Robin & Coco: Castaway Days is a watch face for Wear OS watches and an app for Android phones, made by Peter Logarn and published on Google Play. This policy covers both.

What the watch face collects

Nothing. The watch face does not collect, store on a server, share or sell any personal information or any other data about you. It has:

  • no accounts or sign-in;
  • no advertising;
  • no analytics, tracking or crash reporting;
  • no in-app purchases.

What it shows

To draw the face, your watch's own watch face system gives it values the watch already has: the time and date, your heart rate, your steps and step goal, the weather, how many notifications you haven't read, and the battery level; and, if you turn any on, the complications you choose for it. The face only shows these values on your watch's screen.

The face has no code of its own: it is made only of pictures and a description of the face in Google's Watch Face Format, which your watch draws. So it can't read, keep or send anything anywhere, and it doesn't use the internet.

The settings you choose for the face (such as your wake-up time and bedtime) are kept by your watch, with the face, and never leave it.

The phone app

Robin & Coco's app for your phone is for watching Robin on her island, having it as your wallpaper and putting the face on your watch. It collects nothing about you. It keeps its own settings (such as whether Robin talks) on your phone. To find your watch, it asks your watch's own app on the phone, through Google Play services, which watches are paired; to put the face on the watch, it asks that app to open the Play Store there. Your watch's name stays on your phone.

The app can also show the weather on Robin's island. It's off until you turn it on, and you choose where it's from: a town you type in, or your phone's approximate location (Android asks your permission first, and the app asks for it only while it's open, never in the background). Either way the place is rounded to about a kilometre before it's kept or sent. The app then sends that place, and nothing else about you, to Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com), a weather service, for its forecast: at most about twice an hour, and only while the app, or Robin's island as your wallpaper, is on your screen. A town you type is sent to Open-Meteo's place search, to find it. Like any web service, Open-Meteo sees your phone's IP address with each request; its terms say its logs, which may contain the place, are deleted after 90 days. The place and the forecast are kept only on your phone, and left out of its backups. Turn the weather off in the app's settings and nothing more is sent. The app uses the internet only for this: the link to its privacy policy opens in your browser.

If Robin's island is your phone's wallpaper, the app also notes when your phone's screen turns on and off and when it's unlocked, to count your phone time today and your unlocks, for Robin's jokes about them. Android tells any app that while it runs, with no permission. Those counts are kept only on your phone, left out of its backups, and never sent anywhere. Turn her comments off in the app's settings and nothing more is counted, and what was is forgotten.

Other companies

Neither the watch face nor the phone app has any code for advertising, analytics or tracking.

Your heart rate, steps and weather come from your watch and the apps and services on it, under their own privacy policies; the face only shows them. Google Play, where you get Robin & Coco, has a privacy policy of its own: policies.google.com/privacy.

Open-Meteo, which the phone app asks for the weather if you turn it on, has terms of its own, with what it keeps: open-meteo.com/en/terms.

This website

Robin & Coco's website has no cookies, analytics, advertising or tracking of its own, and its fonts and pictures are its own. Nothing is loaded from any other site until you press play on a film; the film then plays from YouTube (its privacy-enhanced player, youtube-nocookie.com), under Google's privacy policy.

The website is hosted on GitHub Pages, and like any web host, GitHub may record technical details such as your IP address when you visit, under GitHub's privacy statement.

Children

Robin & Coco isn't made for children in particular. It collects no personal information from anyone, of any age.

Changes

If Robin & Coco ever changes in a way that affects this policy, the policy will be updated on this page, with a new date at the top.
